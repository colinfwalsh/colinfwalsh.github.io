## Starting a Technical Blog
For many years I've mulled over creating a technical blog to document my work and learnings in iOS development. Of course, life gets in the way more than I'd like to admit, which has stalled its inception. We're here now though!

### Who Am I
Starting off with an existential question seems appropriate—if you're reading this, you're obviously curious about who I am, but more importantly, I'm trying to figure that out myself. My name is Colin Walsh, and I've been doing iOS development for 9 years now (10 if you count intermittent freelance work). I truly love what I do, and my main goal is to share my knowledge with newer developers interested in this specialty. However, most blogs have covered most topics pretty extensively, so my aim is to discuss case studies and more abstract technical concepts. Hopefully you'll enjoy reading them as much as I enjoy writing them.

### What Makes This Blog Different
While there's no shortage of iOS tutorials covering UIKit basics or SwiftUI fundamentals, I want to explore the messy middle ground—the real-world problems that don't have perfect solutions. Think along the lines of:

- Architecture decisions that went wrong (and what I learned from them)
- Patterns and anti-patterns discovered through painful experience
- Team dynamics and how they shape technical decisions
- Edge cases that turned into production nightmares
- Refactoring stories from legacy codebases

My goal is to share the kind of knowledge you typically only gain through years of experience—the stuff that's hard to Google because you don't even know what questions to ask yet.

### Writing Cadence
I'm committing to one blog post per week. Fair warning: I'm still finding my writing voice, so bear with me while I learn to convey my thoughts more coherently (also I'm going to use AI so don't @ me). Part of this journey is improving my technical writing skills alongside sharing technical knowledge.

### What's Next
In the coming weeks, I'll be covering topics like:

- Why 100% code coverage doesn't make sense
